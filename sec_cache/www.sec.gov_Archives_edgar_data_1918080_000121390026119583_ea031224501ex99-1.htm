PLANO, TX, November 12, 2026 – Deep Isolation Nuclear, Inc. (OTCQB: DBHL) (“Deep Isolation” or the “Company”), a leading innovator in nuclear waste disposal technology, today announced its operational and financial results for the three months ended September 30, 2026.

 

“The third quarter advanced our work toward commercial readiness,” said Rod Baltzer, CEO of Deep Isolation. “We announced results from a three-year DOE-funded study evaluating a permanent disposal pathway for waste from recycled nuclear fuel, and we continued to advance our full-scale, non-radioactive Commercialization Pilot near Cameron, Texas. Subsequent to quarter-end, receipt of the drilling permit from the Texas Railroad Commission and the start of well pad construction marked further steps toward field execution.

 

“Additionally, our common stock began trading on the OTCQB Venture Market in July, and we were selected as the sole industrial partner on three DOE Project GENESIS grants focused on artificial intelligence-enabled repository analysis. We believe the industry’s growing attention to the full nuclear fuel cycle reinforces the importance of developing practical permanent nuclear waste disposal solutions. Our focus remains on demonstrating our technology and working with customers and collaborators to advance its commercial adoption.”

 

 

 

 

Operational Progress

 

During the third quarter, Deep Isolation continued engineering and procurement activities for its non-radioactive, full-scale, at-depth demonstration of its deep borehole disposal technology (the “Commercialization Pilot”) at the Deep Borehole Demonstration Center (“DBDC”), located at Halliburton’s Drilling Technology Facility near Cameron, Texas. The program is designed to test borehole construction, surface handling, and full-scale emplacement and retrieval of the Company’s Universal Canister System (“UCS”). Subsequent to quarter-end, on October 1, 2026, the Company announced that the Texas Railroad Commission had issued a drilling permit, allowing the project to advance toward construction and field activities. The permit is for the non-radioactive pilot and does not authorize disposal of radioactive waste.

 

On October 28, 2026, the Company announced that site preparation, including well pad construction, had begun at the DBDC. The pad will support drilling, casing and cementing operations for the demonstration borehole as part of the Commercialization Pilot. The Commercialization Pilot is being conducted in collaboration with the DBDC, Halliburton, Amentum, NAC International and Occlusion Nuclear Solutions, using non-radioactive packages to simulate repository operations.

 

Commercial Progress and Other Company Events

 

In July 2026, the Company’s common stock was approved for quotation on the OTCQB Venture Market under the symbol “DBHL.” On July 23, 2026, Deep Isolation was selected as the sole industrial partner on three competitive grants under DOE’s Project GENESIS program. Two projects are led by Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory and one by the University of South Carolina, advancing artificial intelligence-enabled repository site screening, design and performance analysis.

 

On September 22, 2026, Deep Isolation announced results from a three-year DOE-funded project under ARPA-E’s CURIE program, conducted with Argonne National Laboratory, Oklo and Case Western Reserve University. Deep Isolation evaluated packaging, repository design, long-term safety and economics for disposal of waste from recycled nuclear fuel. All disposal scenarios studied were projected to cost less than the program’s target of 0.1 cent per kilowatt-hour of electricity generated, and all scenarios under expected conditions met the study’s safety performance criteria.

 

Subsequent to quarter-end, on October 14, 2026, the Company published a customer case study featuring Slovenia’s Agency for Radioactive Waste Management. The assessment compared a standalone disposal facility for TRIGA II research-reactor spent fuel with integration into a proposed deep borehole repository for Krško Nuclear Power Plant spent fuel. The analysis found that integrating the smaller inventory could increase total repository lifecycle costs by less than approximately 0.5%, illustrating the potential benefits of shared disposal infrastructure.

 

Nuclear Industry Developments

 

Permanent nuclear waste disposal remained central to discussions of a sustainable nuclear fuel cycle during the third quarter and subsequent to quarter-end. In the United States, the Department of Energy continued exploring Nuclear Lifecycle Innovation Campuses through voluntary federal-state partnerships that could co-locate enrichment, fuel fabrication, used-fuel reprocessing and waste disposition. Utah, Tennessee, Oklahoma, Louisiana and Idaho signed memoranda of understanding in July. These agreements represent continued exploration of potential host sites, rather than authorization to construct or operate a repository. The initiative highlights the importance of considering permanent disposal alongside investment in nuclear generation and fuel recycling.

 

Internationally, Finland advanced toward permanent nuclear waste disposal in a mined repository when its nuclear safety regulator issued a favorable safety assessment in August. Subsequent to quarter-end, on October 22, the Finnish Government granted Posiva an operating license for its spent-fuel encapsulation and final disposal facility at Olkiluoto, the first such license for a mined repository worldwide. Commissioning tests, inspections and regulatory approvals remain necessary before production operations begin. Fuel-cycle innovation also continued in the United States: in October, ARPA-E selected eight projects for award negotiations under its HORNIG program focused on advanced reactor fuels and the use of recycled nuclear materials. In management’s view, recycling and permanent disposal are complementary elements of long-term waste management, including disposal of the residual waste generated by recycling.

 

Broader nuclear investment continued to reflect demand for reliable electricity, including from artificial intelligence and data centers. Subsequent to quarter-end, Google and Constellation announced a 20-year power purchase agreement on October 6 intended to enable 890 megawatts of additional nuclear capacity through upgrades at 11 existing reactor units. Such investments illustrate the role of existing nuclear facilities in meeting growing power demand while advanced reactor technologies progress toward deployment. In management’s view, continued operation of existing reactors, new nuclear capacity and investment in fuel recycling could expand the long-term need for permanent disposal solutions. Realizing that opportunity will depend on successful technology demonstration, licensing, financing, community engagement and, in the United States, legislative changes needed to allow commercialization of deep borehole disposal.

About Deep Isolation

 

Deep Isolation (OTCQB: DBHL) is the first company to undertake development of technologies for nuclear waste disposal in deep boreholes. Deep Isolation’s solution is designed to offer a unique approach to help countries identify, plan for and complete the necessary steps to dispose of their nuclear waste inventories. With over 100 patents issued to date, Deep Isolation’s technology is designed to leverage proven drilling practices to allow safe isolation of waste deep underground in horizontal, vertical, or slanted borehole repositories. Deep Isolation’s Universal Canister System is engineered to support integrated management of spent fuel and high-level radioactive waste from legacy and advanced reactors across storage, transportation, and eventual disposal. In January 2026, Deep Isolation launched a full-scale, at-depth deep borehole Commercialization Pilot for its solution at Cameron, Texas, in collaboration with the Deep Borehole Demonstration Center, Halliburton (NYSE: HAL), Amentum (NYSE: AMTM), NAC International (TYO: 7004), and Occlusion Nuclear Solutions.

Exhibit II Non-GAAP Financial Measures: EBITDA and Adjusted EBITDA

 

EBITDA is defined as earnings before depreciation, amortization, finance income and expense, and taxes. Adjusted EBITDA is defined as EBITDA before certain items, including registration statement expenses and research and development expenses, that management believes are not indicative of core ongoing operations for the periods presented. EBITDA and Adjusted EBITDA are non-GAAP financial measures that are used as supplemental financial measures by management and external users of financial statements, such as investors, to assess our financial and operating performance. We believe that these non-GAAP financial measures assist our management and investors by increasing the comparability of our performance from period to period. We believe that including EBITDA and Adjusted EBITDA assists our management and investors in (i) understanding and analyzing the results of our operating and business performance, (ii) selecting between investing in us and other investment alternatives and (iii) monitoring our ongoing financial and operational strength in assessing whether to purchase and/or to continue to hold our common shares. This is achieved by excluding the potentially disparate effects between periods of, in the case of EBITDA and Adjusted EBITDA, financial income and expenses,, taxes, depreciation and amortization; in the case of Adjusted EBITDA, registration statement expenses and research and development expenses, which are excluded because they relate to specific, discrete initiatives and may vary significantly based on the timing and scope of such activities.

 

EBITDA and Adjusted EBITDA have limitations as analytical tools and should not be considered as alternatives to, or as substitutes for, or superior to, profit or loss, profit or loss from operations, earnings or loss per share or any other measure of operating performance presented in accordance with GAAP. Some of these limitations include the fact that they do not reflect (i) our cash expenditures or future requirements for capital expenditures or contractual commitments and (ii) changes in, or cash requirements for, our working capital needs. Although depreciation and amortization are non-cash charges, the assets being depreciated and amortized will have to be replaced in the future, and EBITDA and Adjusted EBITDA do not reflect any cash requirements for such replacements. EBITDA and Adjusted EBITDA are not adjusted for all non-cash income or expense items that are reflected in our statements of cash flows and other companies in our industry may calculate these measures differently than we do, limiting their usefulness as a comparative measure.

 

In evaluating Adjusted EBITDA, you should be aware that in the future we may incur expenses that are the same as, or similar to, some of the adjustments in this presentation. Our presentation of Adjusted EBITDA should not be construed as an inference that our future results will be unaffected by the excluded items. Therefore, the non-GAAP financial measures as presented below may not be comparable to similarly titled measures of other companies in the nuclear or other industries.
