NEWS RELEASE

Sylvamo Releases Third Quarter Earnings

MEMPHIS, Tenn. – Nov. 6, 2026 – Sylvamo (NYSE: SLVM), the world’s paper company, is releasing third quarter earnings. The company will host an audio webcast at 10 a.m. EST at investors.sylvamo.com.

Management Summary from Chief Executive Officer John Sims
As we announced earlier this morning, we entered into a put option agreement with Midas Atlantic Partners, in partnership with The Najafi Companies, for a binding offer to acquire our Europe business, including our uncoated freesheet paper mills in Saillat, France, and Nymolla, Sweden, as well as our forestry and supporting business operations.

We believe this potential transaction represents an important strategic step for our Europe business and Sylvamo. In Europe, it would create the opportunity to move forward with an owner experienced in corporate carve-outs and positioning businesses for long-term success. After the proposed transaction, Sylvamo would be a more focused company, with greater ability to invest in our business, strengthen our performance and pursue world-class excellence. We believe that focus will make us a stronger company and better position us to create sustainable value for our employees, customers and investors.

Our long-term strategy and investment thesis is unchanged: we are focusing on uncoated freesheet, investing to strengthen our competitive advantages and leveraging our strengths to generate high returns on invested capital.

Our Europe business is now reported as discontinued operations. Unless otherwise noted, all results and comparisons are for continuing operations excluding Europe. Prior periods have been recast.
-Financial Highlights – Third Quarter
•Net sales from continuing operations were $645 million. Net sales of our European operations, which are reported in discontinued operations, were $178 million in the third quarter of 2026.
•Net income from continuing operations of $85 million ($2.13 per diluted share), a 13% margin, while our discontinued operations reported a $284 million loss, primarily driven by a noncash impairment charge
•Adjusted operating earnings* of $53 million ($1.33 per diluted share) from continuing operations
•Adjusted EBITDA* of $128 million, a 20% margin, from continuing operations
•Cash provided by operating activities from continuing operations of $70 million and free cash flow* of $16 million
•Continuing operations include approximately $6 million to $7 million per quarter of corporate costs previously allocated to our European operations
•We declared a dividend of $0.45 per share, which we paid Oct. 16
-Commercial and Operational Highlights from Continuing Operations – Third Quarter vs. Second Quarter
•Price and mix improved by $25 million, primarily from previously communicated paper price increases
•Volume was relatively flat
•Operations and other costs were favorable by $6 million from improved operational performance
•Planned maintenance outage costs were $38 million lower, as expected
•Input and transportation costs were unfavorable by $6 million, primarily due to higher transportation costs







*See “Non-GAAP Financial Measures” for definitions of non-GAAP financial measures. Reconciliations are included in the financial schedules below.

-Eastover Strategic Investments
Our teams continue to make good progress on our high-return strategic investments at our Eastover mill.

•The paper machine optimization project is underway. The project, which remains on schedule and within budget, will add an additional 60,000 short tons of uncoated freesheet capacity annually.

•We made a strategic decision to keep our three existing cutsize sheeters operational and add a new state-of-the-art sheeter rather than replacing one. The new sheeter will add approximately 200,000 short tons of cutsize converting capacity, with startup expected early in the third quarter of 2027.

•In addition, the woodyard softwood line and the warehouse expansion at our sheeting plant remain on track to start up in the first quarter of 2027
-Regional Business Conditions
•In Latin America, demand is seasonally stronger in the second half of the year. In September, Colombia canceled its antidumping duties on uncoated freesheet imports from Brazil, which restores access to a market we previously served.

•In North America, the industry supply and demand balance remains healthy and our paper price increases are mostly implemented with our customers

•Imports in both regions continue to increase year over year

•Supply chain pressures are driving higher input and transportation costs. Lower supply availability and higher fuel costs affected ocean transportation, while elevated diesel prices and reduced driver availability increased trucking costs in the U.S. Input costs, including chemicals, energy and fiber, continue to be impacted by persistently high oil prices.
-Looking Ahead

In January, I committed that we would allocate capital wisely, focus on long-term value creation and communicate transparently. The proposed sale of our Europe business will make Sylvamo a more focused company, concentrated in Latin America and North America, where we see the greatest opportunities to create value. Our Eastover strategic investments are progressing, our lean transformation is improving performance across our mills and our flagship growth strategy and disciplined capital allocation principles are unchanged. We will continue to invest in low-risk, high-return projects in uncoated freesheet, maintain a strong balance sheet and return cash to shareowners.

About Sylvamo
Sylvamo Corporation (NYSE: SLVM) is the world's paper company with mills in Europe, Latin America and North America. Our vision is to be the employer, supplier and investment of choice. We transform renewable resources into papers that people depend on for education, communication and entertainment. Headquartered in Memphis, Tennessee, we employ more than 6,500 colleagues. Non-GAAP Financial Measures

Adjusted Operating Earnings (Loss) (non-GAAP) are net income (loss) from continuing operations (GAAP), plus the impact of foreign exchange on an intercompany note receivable from our Brazilian subsidiary, and, when applicable for the periods reported, net special items. Management uses this measure to focus on ongoing operations and believes it is useful to investors because it enables them to perform meaningful comparisons of past and present operating results. The Company believes that using this information, along with net income (loss), provides for a more complete analysis of the results of operations. Net income (loss) is the most directly comparable GAAP measure. For more information regarding net special items, see the information under the heading Effects of Net Special Items and the Consolidated Statement of Operations and related notes included later in this release.

Adjusted EBITDA (non-GAAP) is net income (loss) from continuing operations (GAAP), plus the sum of income taxes, net interest expense, depreciation, amortization and cost of timber harvested, stock-based compensation, the impact of foreign exchange on an intercompany note receivable from our Brazilian subsidiary, and, when applicable for the periods reported, net special items. Management uses these measures in managing the operating performance of our business and believes that adjusted EBITDA along with adjusted EBITDA margin provide investors and analysts meaningful insights into our operating performance and is a relevant metric for the third-party debt. Adjusted EBITDA is reconciled to net income (loss), the most directly comparable GAAP measure. Adjusted EBITDA margin (adjusted EBITDA divided by net sales) is reconciled to net income (loss) from continuing operations margin (net income (loss) divided by net sales), the most directly comparable GAAP measure. For more information regarding net special items, see the information under the heading Effects of Net Special Items and the Consolidated Statement of Operations and related notes included later in this release.

Business Segment Operating Profit (Loss) (non-GAAP) is net income (loss) from continuing operations (GAAP), plus the sum of income taxes, net interest expense, the impact of foreign exchange on an intercompany note receivable from our Brazilian subsidiary, and, when applicable for the periods reported, net special items. We believe that business segment operating profit (loss) is an important indicator of operating performance as it is a measure reported to our management for purposes of making decisions about allocating resources to our business segments and assessing the performance of our business segments. For more information regarding net special items, see the information under the heading Effects of Net Special Items and the Consolidated Statement of Operations and related notes included later in this release.

Free Cash Flow is a non-GAAP measure and the most directly comparable GAAP measure is cash provided by operating activities from continuing operations. Management utilizes this measure in connection with managing our business and believes that Free Cash Flow is useful to investors as a liquidity measure because it measures the amount of cash generated that is available, after reinvesting in the business, to maintain a strong balance sheet and service debt, and return cash to shareowners. It should not be inferred that the entire Free Cash Flow amount is available for discretionary expenditures. Free Cash Flow also enables investors to perform meaningful comparisons between past and present periods.
