“In the third quarter of 2026, Five Star raised approximately $130.0 million in net proceeds through an underwritten public offering of 3,133,750 shares of common stock, expanding our capacity for organic growth and strategic investment. Financial highlights as of and during the three months ended September 30, 2026 included the following:
•The Company completed an underwritten public offering of 3,133,750 shares of common stock during the three months ended September 30, 2026, with net proceeds of approximately $130.0 million after deducting underwriting discounts and commissions of approximately $7.3 million, and offering expenses of approximately $0.6 million. The transaction strengthened the Company’s capital base and positioned it for continued organic balance sheet growth, with a common equity Tier 1 capital ratio of 12.52% as of September 30, 2026, as compared to 9.97% as of June 30, 2026. The Bank continues to meet all requirements to be considered “well-capitalized” under applicable regulatory guidelines.
•Basic earnings per share reached $0.92 for the three months ended September 30, 2026, an increase of $0.01 from the second quarter of 2026. Weighted average basic common shares outstanding for the three months ended September 30, 2026 increased by 2,346,467, or 11.03%, as compared to the second quarter of 2026 due to the underwritten public offering. As compared to the three months ended September 30, 2025, basic earnings per share increased $0.15, while weighted average basic common shares outstanding increased by 2,388,806, or 11.25%, as compared to the third quarter of 2025 due to the underwritten public offering.
•Total deposits increased by $25.1 million, or 0.52%, during the three months ended September 30, 2026, with growth in non-wholesale deposits exceeding declines in wholesale deposits. The Company defines wholesale deposits as brokered deposits and California Time Deposit Program deposits. During the three months ended September 30, 2026, non-wholesale deposits increased by $75.1 million, or 1.65%, and wholesale deposits decreased by $50.0 million, or 20.00%.
•The number of Business Development Officers increased from 45 at June 30, 2026 to 50 at September 30, 2026.
•Cash and cash equivalents were $679.5 million, representing 14.08% of total deposits at September 30, 2026, as compared to 14.27% at June 30, 2026.
•Consistent, disciplined management of expenses contributed to our efficiency ratio of 39.13% for the three months ended September 30, 2026, as compared to 40.91% for the three months ended June 30, 2026 and 40.13% for the three months ended September 30, 2025.
•Net interest margin for the three months ended September 30, 2026 expanded six basis points to 3.69% from 3.63% for the three months ended June 30, 2026, primarily reflecting growth in higher-yielding loans and an increase in the yield on interest-earning assets, partially offset by higher deposit funding costs. On a year-over-year basis, net interest margin expanded 13 basis points from 3.56% for the three months ended September 30, 2025 to 3.69% for the three months ended September 30, 2026, supported by balance sheet growth and lower deposit funding costs following three Federal Reserve rate cuts that began in September 2025, partially offset by lower yields on interest-earning assets. The effective federal funds rate declined 21 basis points, from 4.09% as of September 30, 2025 to 3.88% as of September 30, 2026.
•Other comprehensive loss was $3.0 million during the three months ended September 30, 2026, as compared to other comprehensive income of $0.7 million for the three months ended June 30, 2026 and other comprehensive income of $2.1 million for the three months ended September 30, 2025. Unrealized losses, net of tax effect, on available-for-sale securities were $12.4 million as of September 30, 2026, as compared to $9.4 million as of June 30, 2026 and $9.9 million as of September 30, 2025. Total carrying value of held-to-maturity and available-for-sale securities represented 0.04% and 1.52% of total interest-earning assets, respectively, as of September 30, 2026.

Net interest income during the three months ended September 30, 2026 increased by $4.8 million, or 10.51%, to $50.9 million, as compared to $46.1 million during the three months ended June 30, 2026. Net interest margin totaled 3.69% for the three months ended September 30, 2026, an increase of six basis points compared to the prior quarter, primarily reflecting growth in higher-yielding loans and an increase in the yield on interest-earning assets, partially offset by higher deposit funding costs. The $4.8 million increase in net interest income is primarily attributable to a $376.7 million, or 7.39%, increase in average interest-earning assets. The average balance of loans increased $284.3 million, or 6.57%, and the average balance of interest-earning deposits in banks increased $95.7 million, or 14.14%. The average yield on interest-earning assets increased five basis points to 5.74%, including a six-basis-point increase in the average loan yield to 6.14%. The increase in interest income was partially offset by a $2.0 million increase in interest expense. The average balance of deposits increased $257.9 million, or 5.55%, combined with a three-basis-point increase in the average cost of deposits. The average balance of non-interest bearing deposits increased $48.1 million, or 4.13%, helping to partially offset the rise in deposit funding costs.
As compared to the three months ended September 30, 2025, net interest income during the three months ended September 30, 2026 increased by $11.6 million, or 29.42%, to $50.9 million from $39.3 million. Net interest margin totaled 3.69% for the three months ended September 30, 2026, an increase of 13 basis points compared to the same quarter of the prior year. The increase primarily reflected balance sheet growth and lower deposit funding costs following three Federal Reserve rate cuts that began in September 2025, partially offset by lower yields on interest-earning assets. The increase in net interest income is primarily attributable to a $14.3 million increase in interest income, mainly due to a $780.8 million, or 20.38%, increase in the average balance of loans and a $321.4 million, or 71.17%, increase in the average balance of interest-earning deposits in banks. The benefit from higher average earning assets was partially offset by a 13-basis-point decrease in the yield on interest-earning assets to 5.74%. The average loan yield remained stable at 6.14%, as the effect of lower rates on repricing loans was offset by loan growth and changes in portfolio mix. This increase in interest income was partially offset by a $2.8 million increase in interest expense, stemming from a $946.6 million, or 23.89%, increase in the average balance of deposits during the three months ended September 30, 2026, moderated by a 25-basis-point decrease in the average cost of deposits compared to the same quarter of the prior year, reflecting deposit repricing following the three Federal Reserve rate cuts that began in September 2025 and an improved funding mix. Further supporting the decreasing average cost of deposits, the average balance of non-interest-bearing deposits increased by $195.5 million, or 19.23%, and brokered deposits decreased from $216.4 million to zero compared to the same period of the prior year.

Non-GAAP Reconciliation (Unaudited)
The Company uses financial information in its analysis of the Company’s performance that is not in conformity with accounting principles generally accepted in the United States of America (“GAAP”). The Company believes that these non-GAAP financial measures provide useful information to management and investors that is supplementary to the Company’s financial condition, results of operations, and cash flows computed in accordance with GAAP. However, the Company acknowledges that its non-GAAP financial measures have a number of limitations. As such, investors should not view these disclosures as a substitute for results determined in accordance with GAAP. Additionally, these non-GAAP measures are not necessarily comparable to non-GAAP financial measures that other banking companies use. Other banking companies may use names similar to those the Company uses for the non-GAAP financial measures the Company discloses, but may calculate them differently. Investors should understand how the Company and other companies each calculate their non-GAAP financial measures when making comparisons.
Tangible shareholders’ equity to tangible assets is defined as total equity less goodwill and other intangible assets, divided by total assets less goodwill and other intangible assets. The most directly comparable GAAP financial measure is total shareholders’ equity to total assets. Management believes that tangible shareholders’ equity to tangible assets is a useful financial measure because it enables management, investors, and others to assess the Company’s financial health based on tangible capital. We had no goodwill or other intangible assets at the end of any period indicated. As a result, tangible shareholders’ equity to tangible assets is the same as total shareholders’ equity to total assets at the end of each of the periods indicated.
Tangible book value per share is defined as total shareholders’ equity less goodwill and other intangible assets, divided by the outstanding number of common shares at the end of the period. The most directly comparable GAAP financial measure is book value per share. Management believes that tangible book value per share is a useful financial measure because it enables management, investors, and others to assess the Company’s value and use of equity. We had no goodwill or other intangible assets at the end of any period indicated. As a result, tangible book value per share is the same as book value per share at the end of each of the periods indicated.
Pre-tax, pre-provision income is defined as pre-tax income plus provision for credit losses. The most directly comparable GAAP financial measure is pre-tax income. Management believes that pre-tax, pre-provision income is a useful financial measure because it enables management, investors, and others to assess the Company’s ability to generate operating profit and capital.
