Firefly Aerospace Announces Third Quarter 2026 Financial Results With Record Backlog of $1.7 Billion

 

Completed cleanroom expansion to scale Moon Base spacecraft production, signed first European launch contracts, while ramping up Alpha Block II manufacturing and executing across multiple national security programs of record.

 

A Miranda engine test firing in September 2026 at Firefly's Rocket Ranch in Briggs, Texas. Credit: Firefly Aerospace

 

Cedar Park, Texas, November 10, 2026 – Firefly Aerospace (Nasdaq: FLY), a market leading space and defense technology company, today issued financial results for the third quarter ended September 30, 2026.

"Firefly’s third quarter reflects the power of our diversified and vertically-integrated business," said Jason Kim, CEO of Firefly Aerospace. "With five Moon missions running in parallel, Alpha Block II moving toward launch, SciTec executing on critical national security programs, and our strategic deal with AST SpaceMobile, we are converting our pipeline and backlog into results."

 

 

 

Third Quarter 2026 Highlights

•
Revenue of $110.7 million, up 259% from the same period a year prior.
•
Record backlog of $1.7 billion at quarter end, up from $1.5 billion at the end of the prior quarter.
•
Completed expansion and began operating in the Company’s spacecraft cleanroom facility, funded through a non-dilutive Texas Space Commission grant, which enables simultaneous assembly and integration of up to 12 lunar landers and orbital vehicles.
•
Awarded Firefly's first Mars award, with a $13 million subcontract to support the SkyFall mission for NASA JPL, to manufacture, test, and deliver the aeroshell ahead of launch targeted for late 2028.
•
Signed an extension to Lockheed Martin's multi-launch agreement for up to 25 Alpha launches through 2031 using the Block II configuration.
•
Signed with SSC Space for two commercial Alpha launches from Esrange Space Center in Sweden, representing Firefly’s first European launch contracts.
•
Awarded a $94 million Space Force contract under the Ground-Based Radar Digitization program, to support work overhauling legacy defense systems.
•
Onboarded to the $981 million Space Force IDIQ contract for NITE-STAR – the National Space Test and Training Complex Innovative Technology and Engineering - Space Test and Range Capability Development, to compete for task orders to support advanced test and training operations through 2032.
•
Awarded a contract from the Defense Innovation Unit to design an Elytra deorbit system, expanding the Elytra mission portfolio into active debris removal and responsible end-of-life orbital services.
•
Onboarded to NASA’s Spacecraft Processing Operations Contract (SPOC), expanding Firefly’s formal partnership with NASA to provide spacecraft processing and operational support services.
•
Signed a commercial partnership with Starcloud to demonstrate advanced AI compute in lunar orbit, marking Firefly’s first commercial customer for data center capabilities around the Moon.
•
Added Zeno Power Systems as a commercial payload customer for the accelerated Blue Ghost mission to the Moon's near side, to deliver a Survive-the-Night Package with a Radioisotope Heater Unit (RHU).

 

Additional Recent Highlights

•
Signed contracts valued at more than $1.3 billion with AST SpaceMobile, which includes licensed IP, engine delivery, and engineering support.

 

 

•
Onboarded as a vendor to NASA's $10 billion CLPS 2 initiative, to compete for and deliver task orders for large cargo landers to deliver critical infrastructure needed for the agency's Moon Base.
•
Signed Japanese synthetic aperture radar satellite company Synspective to a multi-launch agreement for up to 10 Alpha launches through 2030.
•
Began Miranda engine qualification campaign, with more than 170 hot fire tests completed to date, a key step toward the first Eclipse launch.
•
Extended the U.S. Air Force contract for SciTec's work on Cloud‑Based Command and Control (CBC2) to integrate a new class of counter unmanned aerial system (CUAS) sensor feeds for the Advanced Battle Management System.
•
Completed the MoonFall Preliminary Design Review, verifying the Elytra spacecraft's design to support the flagship NASA mission to the Moon’s south pole.
•
Achieved a FORGE operational milestone for the U.S. Space Force system developed by SciTec, incorporating data from the Space Systems Command's Wide Field of View space vehicle in geostationary Earth orbit (GEO) and expanding the system’s observational reach to strengthen missile warning and defense.
•
Completed the critical design review for Blue Ghost's mission to the South Pole, progressing development of the spacecraft for the NASA flight.
•
Awarded a third hypersonic task order for Alpha from a confidential customer.

 

2026 Full-Year Guidance

•
Firefly expects 2026 full-year revenue to be between $440 million and $450 million.

About Firefly Aerospace

Firefly Aerospace is a space and defense technology company on a mission to reliably and repeatedly launch, land, and operate space systems from Earth to the Moon and beyond. As the partner of choice for responsive space missions, Firefly is the first commercial company to launch a satellite to orbit with approximately 24-hour notice and the first to achieve a successful Moon landing. Firefly’s launch, lunar, and orbital vehicles paired with its AI software and data processing capabilities support end-to-end missions across cislunar space, including payload delivery and maneuverability, space domain awareness, and missile warning and tracking.

 

 

Established in 2017, Firefly’s engineering, manufacturing, and test facilities are co-located in central Texas to enable rapid innovation and vertical integration across the company’s product lines. For more information, visit www.fireflyspace.com. Firefly utilizes its website as a means to distribute material information about the company to the public.

Adjusted EBITDA

We define Adjusted EBITDA as net loss, adjusted for provision for income taxes, interest income, interest expense, depreciation and amortization, stock-based compensation expense, change in fair value of warrant liability, loss on extinguishment of debt, certain one-time costs related to the IPO, transaction-related expenses, gain on settlement of contingent liabilities, and certain other items that are not expected to recur in the future or that management does not view as reflective of the performance of the business. In addition to net loss, we use Adjusted EBITDA to evaluate our business, measure its performance, and make strategic decisions.

We believe that Adjusted EBITDA provides useful information to management, investors, and analysts in assessing our financial performance and results of operations across reporting periods by excluding items we do not believe are indicative of our core operating performance. Net loss is the U.S. GAAP measure most directly comparable to Adjusted EBITDA. Adjusted EBITDA should not be considered as an alternative to net loss. Our presentation of Adjusted EBITDA should not be construed as an inference that our future results will be unaffected by unusual or non-recurring items.

 

 

Free Cash Flow

We define Free Cash Flow as net cash used in operating activities, less purchases of property and equipment and internal-use software. We believe that Free Cash Flow is a meaningful indicator of liquidity that provides information to management and investors about the amount of cash generated from or used in operations, after purchases of property and internal-use software, that (after any debt service requirements or other non-discretionary expenditures not otherwise deducted from the measure) can be used for strategic initiatives, including continuous investment in our business and strengthening our balance sheet.

Free Cash Flow has limitations as a liquidity measure, and you should not consider it in isolation or as a substitute for analysis of our cash flows as reported under U.S. GAAP. Free Cash Flow may be affected in the near to medium term by the timing of capital investments, fluctuations in our growth and the effect of such fluctuations on working capital, and our changes in our cash conversion cycle.
